### 読解パッセージ

When Super Typhoon Bavi slammed into the island of Rota with sustained winds of nearly 290 km/h (180 mph) and gusts approaching 350 km/h, it became one of the most powerful storms ever to strike territory under the American flag. Rota, part of the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands, is home to roughly two thousand people, most of whom rode out the storm in concrete shelters and reinforced homes built after previous typhoons. Although early reports suggested that these structures largely withstood the assault, the island's power grid and water systems were knocked out, leaving residents to face a difficult period of recovery.

What alarmed meteorologists most was the speed at which Bavi strengthened. The storm underwent what forecasters call rapid intensification, vaulting from a modest typhoon to a top-category monster in little more than a day. Such explosive growth is fueled by unusually warm sea surface waters, which supply the heat energy that powers tropical cyclones. Climate scientists say that as the oceans absorb more heat, the conditions favoring rapid intensification may become more common, although they are careful to note that linking any individual storm to global warming requires rigorous attribution studies. The phenomenon also poses a practical danger: when storms strengthen abruptly, forecasters have less lead time to warn communities, and evacuation windows shrink.

Bavi has also renewed attention to the vulnerability of America's remote Pacific territories. When Typhoon Yutu devastated the nearby islands of Tinian and Saipan in 2018, recovery dragged on for years, in part because relief supplies had to be shipped across thousands of kilometers of open ocean. The territories send no voting members to Congress, and critics contend that federal disaster assistance for them has historically been slower and less generous than aid delivered to the fifty states. This time, however, the Federal Emergency Management Agency pre-positioned water, food, and generators on the islands before landfall, a move widely interpreted as a lesson drawn from the shortcomings of the Yutu response.

The storm has reignited debate over how these exposed communities should adapt. Some local officials advocate hardening infrastructure by burying power lines and tightening building codes, while others counter that such projects are prohibitively expensive for islands with small tax bases and must therefore depend on federal support. Yet the arithmetic of repeated disasters suggests that investing in resilience before a storm arrives, however costly, could ultimately prove far cheaper than rebuilding after each catastrophe. Bavi's ferocity is a reminder that, for the Pacific territories, preparedness may be the wiser course than reaction.

**問2. 正解: C（inconspicuous）**

**問3. 正解: C（dispel）**

【正解】'assuring investors that the nation's foreign reserves remained more than sufficient'という保証行為によって'mounting fears'を晴らすという文脈から、不安や疑念を説明・行動で払いのけるdispelが最適。根拠を示して人々の心中の霧を晴らすというdispel固有のニュアンスが合致する。【A: efface】パターンB「意味が近いが文脈の焦点がズレる」─「消す」という点で近いが、effaceは記憶・痕跡・文字などを物理的・比喩的に消し去る語であり、保証によって不安を解消するという心理的働きかけの焦点とズレる。【B: lurk】パターンB「意味が近いが文脈の焦点がズレる」─fearsと共起しやすい語だが、「（不安が）潜む」という自動詞であり、不安を取り除く他動詞の位置には入らない。【C: condone】パターンC「文脈と無関係」─「容認する・大目に見る」の意で、不安を解消する文脈に合わない。【紛らわしいペア】dispel vs efface：dispelは疑念・恐怖を説得や事実で散らす語、effaceは痕跡や記憶を消し去る語である。

**問4. 正解: B（obsequious）**
